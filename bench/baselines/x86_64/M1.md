## Reading these

About 1M nodes/s for a *fully general* engine with no bitboard-specific move
generation, no magic tables, and legality by make/unmake. A dedicated 8x8 engine is
one to two orders of magnitude faster; the gap is the price of generality so far,
and most of it is recoverable:

- `isAttacked` dominates. At ~1.25M queries/s it costs roughly as much as
  everything else combined, because it walks every atom of every piece type. An
  incremental attack map, or a per-variant table of "which atoms can possibly reach
  this cell", is the obvious next step (introduce it behind the oracle differential
  test, never before).
- Legality is make/unmake for every pseudo-legal move. Pin detection would remove
  most of that work.
- Nothing here is vectorized and the dimension-dispatch kernels of M2 do not exist
  yet, so 2-D pays for generality it does not use.

These are the numbers to beat, not numbers to be proud of. The reason they are
recorded now is so that the M2 dimension dispatch can be shown not to have made 2-D
slower, which is an explicit M2 gate.
