# M17.12 — The shapes above two dimensions: what can be a board, and what cannot

Companion to [`M17-geometry-view.md`](M17-geometry-view.md), §M17.12. Records the first
implementation (the `torus3d` and `hyper4` shapes) and the feasibility study the plan asked
for on the `t6` quintic.

## What "playable in the same way" requires

The 2-D geometry view works because `PlaySurface` produces three things off one sampling
and they cannot disagree:

1. **a patch per cell** - the square as a piece of the surface, drawn as a mesh;
2. **a seat per cell** - where a piece stands and which way is up;
3. **a picker** - the ray tested against the same tiles, nearest hit wins.

plus the **adjacency invariant**: two cells adjacent in the lattice are adjacent in the
drawing, and two that are not, are not. A shape that breaks it is a picture, not a board.

For a surface (2 real dimensions) embedded in the 3-D world, all of this is honest. Above
two dimensions the drawing is a *projection* of a higher-dimensional lattice into 3-D, and
that is where it gets hard.

## The dimension problem

A `d`-dimensional manifold cannot be embedded in 3-D once `d > 3`, and even `d = 3` cannot
be embedded *flatly* (a flat 3-torus has no isometric embedding in R³). So every shape
above two dimensions in this project is a **self-intersecting immersion**: distinct cells
land on top of one another. That has three consequences:

- **Coincident tiles.** Distinguishing two cells that project to the same point needs
  something other than position (a shell index, a slice, a ghost).
- **Hidden tiles.** The picker returns the nearest sheet, so a cell behind another is
  unreachable by a click - which is correct for a picture and wrong for a board that must
  let you select *that* cell.
- **Adjacency.** Lattice neighbours that differ only along a folded axis may not be near
  each other in the drawing.

None of this makes the shapes useless; it makes them **deliberate modes** whose limits must
be stated, not defaults.

## The first implementation: `torus3d` and `hyper4`

Built, keyed by the variant's own name (`playShapePosition`, `src/render/overture_scene.cpp`)
because a shape above two dimensions is *authored*, not read off the gluing - `hyper4` is a
plain 4-D box with no identifications at all.

- **`torus3d` — nested shells.** `shellTube` at the point the library screen has fully
  rolled: the file and rank gluings close a T² surface (the shell), and the level axis
  becomes the shell radius, so four levels are four nested shells about one core circle.
  The file/rank adjacency is carried faithfully by the shell; the **level** adjacency is
  carried by *which shell*, which reads clearly but is not Euclidean nearness.
- **`hyper4` — the tesseract.** The 4-cube projected the standard way, the aeon axis being
  the nesting. The inner and outer cubes overlap; picking takes the nearest sheet.

`PlaySurface::buildStacked` places one flat tile per cell from `playShapePosition`, sized
from its neighbours, and the picker tests each tile. Tests: a tile and a seat per cell, unit
normals, a non-degenerate extent, a pick round-trip, and validation-clean GPU renders
(`gui-geometry-torus3d`, `gui-geometry-hyper4`).

**Known limits of this cut.** The level (torus3d) and aeon (hyper4) axes are not adjacent-
in-the-drawing; INVERT and slide are not offered for these shapes yet (`buildStacked`
ignores the pose); and the hidden-sheet problem is unaddressed. They are a first pass, not
the finished shapes.

## The `t6` quintic: the honest answer

The plan asked whether the Calabi-Yau quintic (`src/render/quintic.hpp`) could carry the T⁶
lattice as a playable manifold. **It cannot, and the reason is dimension, not taste.**

- A 6-torus is 6-real-dimensional; the quintic's standard picture
  (`quinticPoint(k1, k2, x, y) -> 3D`) is a projection of a 6-real-dimensional object into
  3-D. It cannot be injective.
- The `t6` overture already demonstrates the fold: it tiles **25** quintic patches
  (`k1, k2 ∈ 0..4`) × **4 × 4** in-sheet cells (`u, v`) = **400** tiles, for the lattice's
  **4⁶ = 4096** cells. That is a ~10:1 collapse - roughly ten lattice cells per drawn tile.
- The quintic therefore carries adjacency along **two** of the six axes (the in-sheet `u`,
  `v`); the other four are folded into the patch index and are not represented as
  adjacency at all. The adjacency invariant fails by construction.

So a playable quintic, in the sense the 2-D surfaces are playable, **does not exist** as a
single 3-D object. The overture's caption already says as much ("the quintic is not this
board's own space").

### What *could* be built, honestly

If a `t6` "shape" is wanted, it must give up the full embedding and take one of:

1. **Keep the lattice (recommended default).** The game already plays `t6` as a grid of
   boards (`view::layout`'s grid axes), which is a faithful, pickable, adjacency-preserving
   view. The quintic stays a library animation. This is M17.4's "extrude the lattice", and
   it is the only *board* here.
2. **A sliced quintic view.** Show a 3-D cross-section of the quintic at a time, with a
   scrub control over the folded axes, so each slice is an injective picture and the picker
   disambiguates within it. This is the same trick the board already uses for the extra
   axes, applied to the quintic: faithful per slice, not globally. Cost: a slice control and
   a per-slice adjacency check.
3. **An interactive immersion with overlap tolerance.** Draw the full collapse and rely on
   nearest-hit picking plus a ghost/slice control to reach hidden cells. Cheap and visual,
   but it is a picture; the interface must say so.

### Verdict

- `torus3d` and `hyper4`: a first shape **is** built and playable, with the level/aeon
  axes' adjacencies stated as limits.
- `t6`: a faithful full embedding is **impossible**; a sliced quintic view is possible and
  honest. Recommended order: finish the 3-D shapes (slide/invert, hidden-sheet handling,
  adjacency tests), then, if a `t6` shape is still wanted, build the **sliced** view (option
  2) rather than the collapse. Do not ship the overture's collapse as a board.

## Finishing the 3-D shapes: slide, invert, a ghost control for the hidden sheet, pinned
## adjacency, and a new twisted variant to prove the gluing language generalises

and labels, before M12.6's runner - see `00-roadmap.md`'s Wave 1 and

### 1. Slide

`PlaySurface::buildStacked` calls `playShapePosition(v, cell, raw)` directly - no pose, no
slide, confirmed by reading the function (`src/render/play_surface.cpp`, ~line 730 onward):
every call is `playShapePosition(v, cell, raw)` with no offset. The 2-D path already
threads `pose.slideU`/`pose.slideV` into its surface functions (see the call sites around
`src/render/overture_scene.cpp` ~line 2641, `kleinSurf(u, v, kTau, 1.0f, kTau, pose.twist,
2.05f * pose.openness, e)` - note `u`/`v` arriving already offset by the caller, not inside
`kleinSurf` itself). Do the same for the stacked path:

- Give `playShapePosition` an overload (or an added `SurfacePose` parameter on the existing
  one - check call sites before choosing) that offsets the file/rank fractions by
  `slideU`/`slideV` (in cells, same units the 2-D pose already uses) before they reach
  `shellTube`: i.e. `u = (file + 0.5f)/4.0f + slideU/4.0f` rather than the bare fraction
  seen at the existing `shellTube` call (~line 2670 of `overture_scene.cpp`). Wrap into
  `[0,1)` the same way the 2-D `wrapSlide` helper (`src/render/play_surface.cpp`) already
  does, reusing that helper rather than a second wrapping implementation.
- `PlaySurface::buildStacked` reads `slideU`/`slideV` off its `SurfacePose` argument
  (already a parameter - it is just not used yet) and forwards them into every
  `playShapePosition` call it makes (`positionAt`'s lambda and the main per-cell loop).
- `hyper4`'s tesseract projection presumably has an equivalent per-axis fractional
  coordinate to offset; find and offset it the same way. If the tesseract's current
  implementation has no natural "slide" (e.g. it is a fixed static projection with no
  periodic axis to rotate), state that explicitly rather than forcing one - `hyper4` has
  no identifications at all (plain 4-D box, per the "first implementation" section above),
  so sliding it may be a no-op by construction, which is a fine, honest answer: confirm and
  record it, do not fake a slide control that does nothing.

### 2. Invert

The 2-D `evert` is already a parameter on `kleinSurf` (the `evert` argument, folded into
`Rr` via `(1.0f - 2.0f * evert)`, ~line 318 of `overture_scene.cpp`) and presumably on the
plain torus surface function the same way. `shellTube` (~line 340) has no such parameter
today. Add one, following the identical pattern: a factor that pulls the shell radius `rr`
through zero and out the other side as `evert` sweeps 0->1, exactly as `kleinSurf`'s `Rr`
does for its own ring radius. Wire `Settings::geometryEvert`/the `INVERT` button through to
it the same way the 2-D path already does (the setting and the button already exist and
already work for the glued-2-D case; this is extending their reach, not building new UI).

### 3. The hidden sheet: reuse GHOST, and a documented picking rule

Nested shells (`torus3d`) and a tesseract's overlapping cubes (`hyper4`) hide cells behind
others by construction (stated as a known limit above). Rather than inventing new hidden-
sheet machinery, extend the **existing** GHOST pipeline (M17.10: `Settings::geometryGhost`,
alpha in `MeshVertex::color.a`, a second blend pass with `depthWriteEnable = false`,
`BoardRenderer::surfaceBlendPipeline_`) to `buildStacked`'s output - the mesh it produces
is built the same way (per-cell patches with vertex colour) as the 2-D surface, so the
renderer's ghost pass should need no change, only `buildStacked` writing an alpha derived
from `geometryGhost` the way `build` (the 2-D path) already does.

Picking still returns the *nearest* hit (`PlaySurface::pick`, Moeller-Trumbore, "nearest
hit wins" - ~line 865 onward), so ghosting an outer shell transparent does not by itself
make the cell behind it clickable; a transparent tile is still the nearest geometric hit.
Fix: when `geometryGhost > 0` on a stacked surface, `pick` should walk *all* ray
intersections (not stop at the first) and return the nearest hit whose patch's effective
alpha (ghost-adjusted) is above some visibility threshold, falling through to the next-
nearest intersected tile otherwise - so clicking through a ghosted outer shell reaches the
one inside it, matching what the player can now see. Document this rule where `pick` is
defined and add the test below.

### 4. A twisted 3-torus: a new variant, to prove the gluing language already generalises

A genuinely new topology, not yet shipped, purely as data if only the lattice is wanted,
and with the shape extended (below) if the trailer wants to *see* the twist. Candidate
name: `torus3d_twist` (or similar - match the existing `atomic_torus` naming convention of
`<base>_<modifier>`).

**The data.** Copy `variants/torus3d.toml`, and give the **rank** identification a
`flip = ["file"]`, exactly as `klein.toml` does to its own rank identification - compare:

```toml
# klein.toml, today
[[geometry.identify]]
axis = "rank"
kind = "periodic"
flip = ["file"]
```

```toml
# torus3d_twist.toml, new - same idea, one more axis along for the ride
[[geometry.identify]]
axis = "file"
kind = "periodic"

[[geometry.identify]]
axis = "rank"
kind = "periodic"
flip = ["file"]

[[geometry.identify]]
axis = "level"
kind = "periodic"
```

**Before writing any shape code: confirm this loads and plays on the flat lattice as-is.**
The identification language's direction-transport is derived, not authored per-axis-count
(M3.1), so there is no obvious reason a `flip` declared on a 3-axis variant should behave
differently from the 2-axis `klein` case - but this has never been shipped, so it is the
first thing to check, with the existing shipped-variant goldens
(`tests/golden/test_shipped_variants.cpp`) as the bar: legal start position (no side in
check), a bishop/rook test analogous to Klein's "a bishop finally escapes its colour" if
the piece set supports it, and a perft/property check same as any new variant
(`cb-new-variant` skill). **If this does not hold cleanly, stop and report it rather than
proceeding to the shape work below** - a 3-D non-orientable gluing bug is a more important
finding than its shape view.

**The shape, if the flat-lattice check above is clean.** `shellTube`'s first revolve
(`xl` from the **file** coordinate, producing the `(a, b)` tube cross-section, ~lines
344-351) is structurally the same plain-circle revolve `kleinSurf` starts from (`cr, ca`
from `u`, ~lines 297-304) before `kleinSurf` pinches it. Since the twist here is declared
on the **rank** identification (crossing the big shell loop reflects the file
cross-section - same relationship as klein's rank-identification-flips-file), the fix is:
before `shellTube`'s *second* (rank-driven, `ph`) revolve, pinch the `(a, b)` pair the same
way `kleinSurf` pinches `(cr, ca)` (~lines 305-312: the lemniscate-angle blend, `pinch`
sweeping the cross-section from a circle to a figure-eight so a half-turn comes back
*reflected* rather than *shifted*, per the doc comment on `kleinSurf` explaining exactly
why a plain circle cannot close under a flip). Write this as a new function (`twistedShell
Tube`, or a `pinch` parameter added to `shellTube` mirroring `kleinSurf`'s own) rather than
duplicating `shellTube` wholesale.

**This is the highest-uncertainty item in this spec.** Getting a self-intersecting
non-orientable 3-D immersion's normal field continuous (the thing M17.18 had to fix twice
for the 2-D Klein bottle - "the one unavoidable flip... off the lemniscate's numerically
unstable pinch... onto the seam the walk does not close") may need the same continuity-walk
treatment `PlaySurface::build` uses for 2-D, adapted to `buildStacked`'s per-cell frame
construction (`axisFrame`'s tangent-difference, ~line 758 onward) rather than a naive
per-cell normal. **Prototype the shape function in isolation first** (a small standalone
check - e.g. a scratch test that calls the new shape function directly across its whole
domain and plots/inspects the result, the way `--shot` of the plain `torus3d` overture was
used to eyeball `shellTube` originally) **before** wiring it into `buildStacked`'s seat/
patch machinery, so a parametrisation bug is caught before it is buried under frame and
picking code. If the normal-continuity problem proves as deep as Klein's did, it is
reasonable to ship the **flat-lattice-only** variant (stop after the "confirm it loads and
plays" step above) and defer the shape to a follow-up - record that decision in this file's
status section rather than shipping a visibly broken shell.

### Tests

- `variants/torus3d_twist.toml` (or chosen name) passes the full shipped-variant golden
  suite (legal start, perft/property sweep, round-trip) like any other variant.
- A piece-colour-escape test analogous to Klein's, if the piece set has a colour-bound
  piece (a bishop) - asserting the twist actually changes reachability versus the
  untwisted `torus3d`.
- Slide: a property test that `buildStacked` at `slideU = k` cells reproduces the untwisted
  (`slideU = 0`) result with the lattice coordinates rotated by `k` - the shape is the same
  shape, differently parked. Covers `torus3d`, `hyper4` (or confirms and records hyper4's
  slide is a no-op), and the new twisted variant.
- Invert: a property test that `evert = 1` and `evert = 0` place the same cell's piece on
  opposite sides of its local tangent plane (mirrors the existing 2-D evert test's
  assertion shape).
- Ghost + picking: with `geometryGhost` above the visibility threshold, a ray that hits an
  outer shell/face and an inner one behind it picks the **inner** cell; with ghost off, the
  same ray picks the **outer** one - one test, two settings, asserting the rule in §3.
  Validation-clean `--shot`/`--ghost` captures on `torus3d` and `hyper4`.
- **Adjacency, pinned rather than asserted in prose.** For `torus3d`: every file/rank-
  adjacent pair of cells (per the engine's own king-move adjacency) has drawn centres
  within some small multiple of a cell's own size on the *same* shell; every level-adjacent
  pair sits on *different* (index-adjacent) shells at the same angular position - assert
  both, so "level adjacency is not Euclidean nearness" is a fact the suite checks, not just
  prose a future change could quietly invalidate (in either direction - silently fixing it
  without updating this note would be just as much a surprise as silently breaking it
  further). Same shape for `hyper4`'s aeon axis.

### Acceptance

1. `torus3d --geometry --shape-follow turntable`-style captures (slide, invert, ghost all
   stated via the existing flags) are validation-clean and show the documented behaviour.
2. The new twisted variant loads, plays correctly on the flat lattice, and passes the
   shipped-variant goldens - independent of whether the shape work in §4 is completed.
3. Picking reaches a ghosted-clear inner cell on both `torus3d` and `hyper4`.
4. `tools/test.sh --build render` green; `nix flake check` clean.

### Risks and non-goals

- `t6` stays lattice-only (per the Verdict above) - nothing here touches the quintic or the
  sliced-view option; that remains a separate, not-yet-scoped piece of work.
- Slide/invert/ghost are presentation; none may enter `VariantId`, matching every other
  pose control in M17.
- If the twisted shape's normal continuity turns out to need research-grade effort beyond
  what §4 budgets, shipping the flat-lattice-only variant and recording the shape as
  deferred is an acceptable, explicitly non-failing outcome - say so in this file's status
  section rather than leaving a broken shell in the library.
