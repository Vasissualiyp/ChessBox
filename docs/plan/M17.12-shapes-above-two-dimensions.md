# M17.12 — The shapes above two dimensions: what can be a board, and what cannot

Companion to [`M17-geometry-view.md`](M17-geometry-view.md), §M17.12. Records the first
implementation (the `torus3d` and `hyper4` shapes) and the feasibility study the plan asked
for on the `t6` quintic.

## What "playable in the same way" requires

The 2-D geometry view works because `PlaySurface` produces three things off one sampling
and they cannot disagree:

1. **a patch per cell** - the square as a piece of the surface, drawn as a mesh;
2. **a seat per cell** - where a piece stands and which way is up;
3. **a picker** - the ray tested against the same tiles, nearest hit wins.

plus the **adjacency invariant**: two cells adjacent in the lattice are adjacent in the
drawing, and two that are not, are not. A shape that breaks it is a picture, not a board.

For a surface (2 real dimensions) embedded in the 3-D world, all of this is honest. Above
two dimensions the drawing is a *projection* of a higher-dimensional lattice into 3-D, and
that is where it gets hard.

## The dimension problem

A `d`-dimensional manifold cannot be embedded in 3-D once `d > 3`, and even `d = 3` cannot
be embedded *flatly* (a flat 3-torus has no isometric embedding in R³). So every shape
above two dimensions in this project is a **self-intersecting immersion**: distinct cells
land on top of one another. That has three consequences:

- **Coincident tiles.** Distinguishing two cells that project to the same point needs
  something other than position (a shell index, a slice, a ghost).
- **Hidden tiles.** The picker returns the nearest sheet, so a cell behind another is
  unreachable by a click - which is correct for a picture and wrong for a board that must
  let you select *that* cell.
- **Adjacency.** Lattice neighbours that differ only along a folded axis may not be near
  each other in the drawing.

None of this makes the shapes useless; it makes them **deliberate modes** whose limits must
be stated, not defaults.

## The first implementation: `torus3d` and `hyper4`

Built, keyed by the variant's own name (`playShapePosition`, `src/render/overture_scene.cpp`)
because a shape above two dimensions is *authored*, not read off the gluing - `hyper4` is a
plain 4-D box with no identifications at all.

- **`torus3d` — nested shells.** `shellTube` at the point the library screen has fully
  rolled: the file and rank gluings close a T² surface (the shell), and the level axis
  becomes the shell radius, so four levels are four nested shells about one core circle.
  The file/rank adjacency is carried faithfully by the shell; the **level** adjacency is
  carried by *which shell*, which reads clearly but is not Euclidean nearness.
- **`hyper4` — the tesseract.** The 4-cube projected the standard way, the aeon axis being
  the nesting. The inner and outer cubes overlap; picking takes the nearest sheet.

`PlaySurface::buildStacked` places one flat tile per cell from `playShapePosition`, sized
from its neighbours, and the picker tests each tile. Tests: a tile and a seat per cell, unit
normals, a non-degenerate extent, a pick round-trip, and validation-clean GPU renders
(`gui-geometry-torus3d`, `gui-geometry-hyper4`).

**Known limits of this cut.** The level (torus3d) and aeon (hyper4) axes are not adjacent-
in-the-drawing; INVERT and slide are not offered for these shapes yet (`buildStacked`
ignores the pose); and the hidden-sheet problem is unaddressed. They are a first pass, not
the finished shapes.

## The `t6` quintic: the honest answer

The plan asked whether the Calabi-Yau quintic (`src/render/quintic.hpp`) could carry the T⁶
lattice as a playable manifold. **It cannot, and the reason is dimension, not taste.**

- A 6-torus is 6-real-dimensional; the quintic's standard picture
  (`quinticPoint(k1, k2, x, y) -> 3D`) is a projection of a 6-real-dimensional object into
  3-D. It cannot be injective.
- The `t6` overture already demonstrates the fold: it tiles **25** quintic patches
  (`k1, k2 ∈ 0..4`) × **4 × 4** in-sheet cells (`u, v`) = **400** tiles, for the lattice's
  **4⁶ = 4096** cells. That is a ~10:1 collapse - roughly ten lattice cells per drawn tile.
- The quintic therefore carries adjacency along **two** of the six axes (the in-sheet `u`,
  `v`); the other four are folded into the patch index and are not represented as
  adjacency at all. The adjacency invariant fails by construction.

So a playable quintic, in the sense the 2-D surfaces are playable, **does not exist** as a
single 3-D object. The overture's caption already says as much ("the quintic is not this
board's own space").

### What *could* be built, honestly

If a `t6` "shape" is wanted, it must give up the full embedding and take one of:

1. **Keep the lattice (recommended default).** The game already plays `t6` as a grid of
   boards (`view::layout`'s grid axes), which is a faithful, pickable, adjacency-preserving
   view. The quintic stays a library animation. This is M17.4's "extrude the lattice", and
   it is the only *board* here.
2. **A sliced quintic view.** Show a 3-D cross-section of the quintic at a time, with a
   scrub control over the folded axes, so each slice is an injective picture and the picker
   disambiguates within it. This is the same trick the board already uses for the extra
   axes, applied to the quintic: faithful per slice, not globally. Cost: a slice control and
   a per-slice adjacency check.
3. **An interactive immersion with overlap tolerance.** Draw the full collapse and rely on
   nearest-hit picking plus a ghost/slice control to reach hidden cells. Cheap and visual,
   but it is a picture; the interface must say so.

### Verdict

- `torus3d` and `hyper4`: a first shape **is** built and playable, with the level/aeon
  axes' adjacencies stated as limits.
- `t6`: a faithful full embedding is **impossible**; a sliced quintic view is possible and
  honest. Recommended order: finish the 3-D shapes (slide/invert, hidden-sheet handling,
  adjacency tests), then, if a `t6` shape is still wanted, build the **sliced** view (option
  2) rather than the collapse. Do not ship the overture's collapse as a board.

### 1. Slide

`PlaySurface::buildStacked` calls `playShapePosition(v, cell, raw)` directly - no pose, no
slide, confirmed by reading the function (`src/render/play_surface.cpp`, ~line 730 onward):
every call is `playShapePosition(v, cell, raw)` with no offset. The 2-D path already
threads `pose.slideU`/`pose.slideV` into its surface functions (see the call sites around
`src/render/overture_scene.cpp` ~line 2641, `kleinSurf(u, v, kTau, 1.0f, kTau, pose.twist,
2.05f * pose.openness, e)` - note `u`/`v` arriving already offset by the caller, not inside
`kleinSurf` itself). Do the same for the stacked path:

- Give `playShapePosition` an overload (or an added `SurfacePose` parameter on the existing
  one - check call sites before choosing) that offsets the file/rank fractions by
  `slideU`/`slideV` (in cells, same units the 2-D pose already uses) before they reach
  `shellTube`: i.e. `u = (file + 0.5f)/4.0f + slideU/4.0f` rather than the bare fraction
  seen at the existing `shellTube` call (~line 2670 of `overture_scene.cpp`). Wrap into
  `[0,1)` the same way the 2-D `wrapSlide` helper (`src/render/play_surface.cpp`) already
  does, reusing that helper rather than a second wrapping implementation.
- `PlaySurface::buildStacked` reads `slideU`/`slideV` off its `SurfacePose` argument
  (already a parameter - it is just not used yet) and forwards them into every
  `playShapePosition` call it makes (`positionAt`'s lambda and the main per-cell loop).
- `hyper4`'s tesseract projection presumably has an equivalent per-axis fractional
  coordinate to offset; find and offset it the same way. If the tesseract's current
  implementation has no natural "slide" (e.g. it is a fixed static projection with no
  periodic axis to rotate), state that explicitly rather than forcing one - `hyper4` has
  no identifications at all (plain 4-D box, per the "first implementation" section above),
  so sliding it may be a no-op by construction, which is a fine, honest answer: confirm and
  record it, do not fake a slide control that does nothing.

### 2. Invert

The 2-D `evert` is already a parameter on `kleinSurf` (the `evert` argument, folded into
`Rr` via `(1.0f - 2.0f * evert)`, ~line 318 of `overture_scene.cpp`) and presumably on the
plain torus surface function the same way. `shellTube` (~line 340) has no such parameter
today. Add one, following the identical pattern: a factor that pulls the shell radius `rr`
through zero and out the other side as `evert` sweeps 0->1, exactly as `kleinSurf`'s `Rr`
does for its own ring radius. Wire `Settings::geometryEvert`/the `INVERT` button through to
it the same way the 2-D path already does (the setting and the button already exist and
already work for the glued-2-D case; this is extending their reach, not building new UI).

### 3. The hidden sheet: reuse GHOST, and a documented picking rule

Nested shells (`torus3d`) and a tesseract's overlapping cubes (`hyper4`) hide cells behind
others by construction (stated as a known limit above). Rather than inventing new hidden-
sheet machinery, extend the **existing** GHOST pipeline (M17.10: `Settings::geometryGhost`,
alpha in `MeshVertex::color.a`, a second blend pass with `depthWriteEnable = false`,
`BoardRenderer::surfaceBlendPipeline_`) to `buildStacked`'s output - the mesh it produces
is built the same way (per-cell patches with vertex colour) as the 2-D surface, so the
renderer's ghost pass should need no change, only `buildStacked` writing an alpha derived
from `geometryGhost` the way `build` (the 2-D path) already does.

Picking still returns the *nearest* hit (`PlaySurface::pick`, Moeller-Trumbore, "nearest
hit wins" - ~line 865 onward), so ghosting an outer shell transparent does not by itself
make the cell behind it clickable; a transparent tile is still the nearest geometric hit.
Fix: when `geometryGhost > 0` on a stacked surface, `pick` should walk *all* ray
intersections (not stop at the first) and return the nearest hit whose patch's effective
alpha (ghost-adjusted) is above some visibility threshold, falling through to the next-
nearest intersected tile otherwise - so clicking through a ghosted outer shell reaches the
one inside it, matching what the player can now see. Document this rule where `pick` is
defined and add the test below.

### 4. A twisted 3-torus: a new variant, to prove the gluing language already generalises

A genuinely new topology, not yet shipped, purely as data if only the lattice is wanted,
and with the shape extended (below) if the trailer wants to *see* the twist. Candidate
name: `torus3d_twist` (or similar - match the existing `atomic_torus` naming convention of
`<base>_<modifier>`).

**The data.** Copy `variants/torus3d.toml`, and give the **rank** identification a
`flip = ["file"]`, exactly as `klein.toml` does to its own rank identification - compare:

```toml

kind = "periodic"
```

```toml
[[geometry.identify]]

kind = "periodic"
```

**Before writing any shape code: confirm this loads and plays on the flat lattice as-is.**
The identification language's direction-transport is derived, not authored per-axis-count

differently from the 2-axis `klein` case - but this has never been shipped, so it is the
first thing to check, with the existing shipped-variant goldens
(`tests/golden/test_shipped_variants.cpp`) as the bar: legal start position (no side in
check), a bishop/rook test analogous to Klein's "a bishop finally escapes its colour" if
the piece set supports it, and a perft/property check same as any new variant
(`cb-new-variant` skill). **If this does not hold cleanly, stop and report it rather than
proceeding to the shape work below** - a 3-D non-orientable gluing bug is a more important
finding than its shape view.

**The shape, if the flat-lattice check above is clean.** `shellTube`'s first revolve
(`xl` from the **file** coordinate, producing the `(a, b)` tube cross-section, ~lines
344-351) is structurally the same plain-circle revolve `kleinSurf` starts from (`cr, ca`
from `u`, ~lines 297-304) before `kleinSurf` pinches it. Since the twist here is declared
on the **rank** identification (crossing the big shell loop reflects the file
cross-section - same relationship as klein's rank-identification-flips-file), the fix is:
before `shellTube`'s *second* (rank-driven, `ph`) revolve, pinch the `(a, b)` pair the same
way `kleinSurf` pinches `(cr, ca)` (~lines 305-312: the lemniscate-angle blend, `pinch`
sweeping the cross-section from a circle to a figure-eight so a half-turn comes back
*reflected* rather than *shifted*, per the doc comment on `kleinSurf` explaining exactly
why a plain circle cannot close under a flip). Write this as a new function (`twistedShell
Tube`, or a `pinch` parameter added to `shellTube` mirroring `kleinSurf`'s own) rather than
duplicating `shellTube` wholesale.

**This is the highest-uncertainty item in this spec.** Getting a self-intersecting
non-orientable 3-D immersion's normal field continuous (the thing M17.18 had to fix twice
for the 2-D Klein bottle - "the one unavoidable flip... off the lemniscate's numerically
unstable pinch... onto the seam the walk does not close") may need the same continuity-walk
treatment `PlaySurface::build` uses for 2-D, adapted to `buildStacked`'s per-cell frame
construction (`axisFrame`'s tangent-difference, ~line 758 onward) rather than a naive
per-cell normal. **Prototype the shape function in isolation first** (a small standalone
check - e.g. a scratch test that calls the new shape function directly across its whole
domain and plots/inspects the result, the way `--shot` of the plain `torus3d` overture was
used to eyeball `shellTube` originally) **before** wiring it into `buildStacked`'s seat/
patch machinery, so a parametrisation bug is caught before it is buried under frame and
picking code. If the normal-continuity problem proves as deep as Klein's did, it is
reasonable to ship the **flat-lattice-only** variant (stop after the "confirm it loads and
plays" step above) and defer the shape to a follow-up - record that decision in this file's
status section rather than shipping a visibly broken shell.

## Revision: the D >= 3 shapes read as flat tiles, not a surface - and hyper4 wants a real fourth-dimension control (2026-10-06)

Reported directly, from actual play: `torus3d` "is not that bad - you can see the
toroidal structure, just need to 'extrapolate' the squares into the smooth manifolds that
are connecting," but `hyper4` "looks just like a bunch of 'fans' stuck together." A
follow-up request: a middle-drag 4D rotation for `hyper4`, and - to cut down the visual
clutter a tesseract's own self-overlap creates - it is "fine to only show 6-7 out of 8
cubes at a time... the outer cube should not be shown." Three pieces, independently
scoped: (A) a real root cause for the flat-tile look, shared by both shapes; (B) and (C)
are `hyper4`-only and build on each other.

### A. Why both shapes read as flat tiles, not a curved manifold

Root cause, confirmed by reading `PlaySurface::buildStacked` (`play_surface.cpp:949`)
against the 2-D path it sits beside: the 2-D glued surface (`PlaySurface::build`) samples
the continuous surface function at a fine `kSubdiv`-step grid *inside* every cell, so a
"square" is actually a curved patch cut from the real manifold - which is the whole reason
`torus`/`klein`/`mobius` read as smooth shapes rather than a chessboard folded into a ring.
`buildStacked` does not do this: `playShapePosition` is called once per cell, at its
centre, and the cell's corners are reconstructed from a tangent-plane approximation
(`axisFrame`, sampling only the two *neighbouring cells'* centres to estimate a tangent) -
one flat, rigid quad per cell, not a sampled patch. For `torus3d`, whose `shellTube`
embedding curves gently from one cell to the next, a flat-quad approximation is a visible
but modest facet - "you can see the structure, just need to extrapolate it," exactly the
report. For `hyper4`, the embedding is a 4D-to-3D *perspective* projection
(`k = 2.35 / (3.5 - nw)`, `playShapePosition`'s hyper4 branch,
`overture_scene.cpp:2730`) - a reciprocal, not a gentle curve - so neighbouring cells'
tangent-plane approximations can diverge sharply in angle near the far side of the
projection, which is almost certainly the reported "bunch of fans stuck together."

**The fix.** Both `shellTube` and the hyper4 projection are already continuous functions
of their own (real-valued) coordinates - `playShapePosition` just only ever evaluates
them at integer cell centres. Give `buildStacked` the same treatment `build` already has:
sample the underlying continuous position at a `kSubdiv`-step sub-grid within each cell
(and, where it matters, across the cell boundary into its neighbour, the way the 2-D
patches already cut a gapless shared edge) instead of one point plus a tangent
extrapolation. This likely wants a continuous-coordinate sibling of `playShapePosition`
(taking fractional axis coordinates rather than a `CellId`) that both the existing
per-cell call and the new sub-grid sampling can share, rather than two separate
derivations of the same `shellTube`/tesseract math.

**Tests.** A property test that adjacent sub-grid samples within one cell move smoothly
(no larger a step than the existing per-cell adjacency check already uses) for both
`torus3d` and `hyper4`. The existing pinned adjacency test (`torus3d` lights its
twenty-six neighbours, etc.) must keep passing unchanged - this is a tessellation density
change, not a topology change.

**Acceptance.** `torus3d --geometry` reads as a smoothly curved nested-shell surface, not
faceted squares; `hyper4 --geometry` no longer reads as disconnected fans - adjacent cells'
patches visibly belong to the same continuous surface.

### Status: A built (2026-10-06, opencode)

**Built.** `PlaySurface::buildStacked` now cuts each cell's tile from the same continuous
surface its centre already comes from, instead of a tangent-plane quad. The shared
derivation is a new `playShapeAt(v, axis, out, pose)` (`overture_scene.cpp`): it takes
fractional lattice coordinates (`axis[k]` in cells, a cell centre at `axis[k] + 0.5`) and is
now the one place `shellTube` and the hyper4 tesseract projection are written.
`playShapePosition` is this function at a whole cell, so the per-cell centre and the new
sub-grid samples cannot drift; at every integer cell it is bit-identical to the old code
(the slide, invert and `formed = 0` tests pass unchanged). `buildStacked` samples the cell's
own `kCoverage`-inset footprint on the 2-D path's `kSubdiv` grid and gives each corner a
normal from the surface's own finite-difference tangents (the same 0.2-cell step `normalAt`
uses), oriented to the seat. The picker and anti-clip test read `patches_` directly
(`pick`/`blocked` iterate the sub-quads, as the 2-D path iterates `corners_`), so ADR-0011
holds on the stacked path; the separate `quads_`/`quadCells_` are gone.

**One deviation, recorded.** The spec calls hyper4 "a reciprocal, not a gentle curve, so
neighbouring cells' tangents diverge". The projection is **projective**, so a fixed
(level, aeon) 4x4 file/rank board maps to a *plane* to machine precision (measured
coplanarity residual ~1e-16, all sixteen sheets) - a hyper4 tile is flat, and a
coplanarity test cannot pin the fix there. What the old tangent quad got wrong on hyper4 is
its *shape and size*: the true cell is a projective trapezoid, and the centred rectangle
misses it by up to **4.4 world units** with file-adjacent gaps up to **0.89**; the sampled
patch is exactly on the surface (0.0) with the intended 0.1-cell coverage gap (max
**0.33**). So the test asserts the property true for both shapes - every patch corner is
the continuous surface's own point at its lattice coordinate, and adjacent sub-grid samples
step by under a cell - rather than the coplanarity assertion, which hyper4's planarity
makes vacuous. The overlapping planes hyper4 still shows are its own level/aeon adjacency,
what B and C exist to tame.

**Tests.** The new `a stacked square is a patch of the surface, not a tangent quad` went red
first, then green; the `torus3d` 26-neighbour and `hyper4` aeon/level adjacency tests pass
unchanged. `picking a three- and four-dimensional shape finds the tile`: the self-agreement
bound moved `hits/5` -> `hits/8` because the real curved/projective tiles occlude more than
the old gapped quads (`torus3d` 10/63, `hyper4` 52/253); the invariant that matters - every
non-self pick is still under the pixel - is unchanged and still checked. `tools/test.sh
--build render` green (129 cases), `--build app` green (68), `tools/precommit.sh` green.

**Visual captures** (`build/m17_12a/`, `--geometry --shot`, 1600x1200). `torus3d` before
was a star of separate flat plates; after it is one smooth nested-shell torus with
continuous shading across every tile boundary - the reported "extrapolate the squares into
the smooth manifolds" is exactly what happened. `hyper4` before, zoomed in, was randomly
misaligned rectangles at slightly different angles; after, adjacent tiles share edges and
form one coherent projective grid per (level, aeon) sheet. It still reads as overlapping
blades because it *is* sixteen intersecting planes at this fixed single-plane spin, but the
disconnected-fan tiling is gone.

### B. A middle-drag 4D rotation for `hyper4`

Currently `playShapePosition`'s hyper4 branch rotates `file`/`aeon` (`cx`/`cw`) by a

### D. A wireframe scaffold: the tesseract's own 16 corners, to anchor the rotation

Reported directly, right after B shipped: "the rotations are quite disorienting." That
tracks - B turns 256 individual cell patches through two independent 4-D planes with
nothing fixed on screen to read the turn *against*. The classic fix for an unreadable
hypercube rotation is the one every tesseract diagram already uses: draw the 4-cube's own
16-vertex, 32-edge skeleton alongside it, so the eye has a small number of long, stable
lines to track instead of 256 little tiles all moving at once. This codebase already
draws exactly that shape, decoratively, for the main-menu field - `deco.cpp`'s
`WireShape::Tesseract`/`tesseractVertices`/`buildWireShape` (16 vertices from the unit
4-cube's corners, turned by two angles, edges by XOR-adjacency: vertex `i` joins `i ^
(1<<d)` for each bit `d`, each pair drawn once via `if (j < i) continue`). That function
is the right algorithm to crib the *topology* from (which of the 16 corners join which),
but it is the wrong code to call: it projects into its own object-local unit cube and
draws straight into an `ImDrawList` on the 2-D menu canvas, nothing like the real board's
3-D Vulkan scene or `hyper4`'s own projection. Build a second, independent instance of
the same topology that goes through the real pipeline instead.

**Build.** The 16 corners are the hypercube's true geometric corners - not cell centres -
so call `playShapeAt(v, axis, out, pose)` directly (the exact function B's rotation lives
in) with `axis[k]` set to the extreme `{0.0f, 4.0f}` for each of the four dims (since that
branch computes `cx = axis[0] - 2.0f` etc., `{0, 4}` is `{-2, +2}`, the corners just
outside the outermost cell centres at `{0.5, 3.5}` i.e. `{-1.5, +1.5}` - the scaffold
should enclose the cells, not pass through their centres). This reuses B's rotation and
projection exactly, with no duplicated formula to drift: the scaffold turns in lockstep
with the board because it *is* the board's own math, evaluated at 16 fixed lattice
corners instead of 256 cell centres. Generate the 32 edges with the same XOR-adjacency
`deco.cpp` uses (`i ^ (1 << d)`, `j < i` skipped) - that part of the algorithm is exactly
transferable, only the vertex positions and the output format differ.

Each edge becomes one thin bar `Instance` on `Archetype::Cell` (the same archetype the
timeline rail bars and the capture-flourish ring already reuse for "a thin shape in an
arbitrary pose," read `byShape[Archetype::Cell].push_back(...)` nearby in
`buildInstances` for the pattern), pushed alongside the pieces in the same `surface`
branch of `BoardRenderer::buildInstances` (`src/render/board_renderer.cpp`, the
`options_.surface && !options_.flat && hasPlaySurface(v)` block), gated additionally on
`v.name == "hyper4"` - this scaffold is specific to the tesseract, not every D>=3 shape.
Unlike the timeline rails, a hypercube edge runs in an arbitrary 3-D direction, not along
a board axis, so centring and scaling along the segment is not enough - the bar's long
axis has to be turned onto the edge's own direction. Do not invent that from scratch:
`quatOf(ex, ey, normal)` (`src/render/play_surface.cpp:56`, already used to build
`seat.quat` for a piece standing on the surface) builds a quaternion from an explicit
orthonormal frame, and is the same tool needed here - pick any two unit vectors
perpendicular to the edge direction as the other two axes (their own orientation around
the bar does not matter, since a bar is symmetric about its own length). Keep the
scaffold visually quiet: a muted, already-existing theme tone (`theme_.boardRim`, the rim
tint the patches already groove-shade with, is a reasonable fit) at a modest alpha, thin
enough to read as a wire rather than a strut. Gate it on `options_.showSeams` - the
player's existing toggle for "structural, not gameplay" markings on the shape view -
rather than adding a new setting; state plainly if a separate toggle is judged better and
why.

**Tests.** A pure-geometry test: at the default (undragged) pose, the 16 projected
corners form a shape consistent with "two nested cubes" (e.g. 8 of them, at one `nw`/`nd`
depth band, project to a strictly smaller silhouette than the other 8 - however this
plan's part C already characterises "inner" vs "outer," reuse that same characterisation
rather than inventing a second one). The 32-edge XOR-adjacency count and connectivity
(every vertex has exactly 4 edges, matching a 4-cube) is checked combinatorially, not
visually. A continuity check matching B's own: a small turn of either `hyperSpin` angle
moves every corner by well under a cell's own spacing, so the scaffold never jumps.

**Acceptance.** `hyper4 --geometry` shows a recognisable two-nested-cubes wire scaffold
around the stack of cell patches, both at rest and while middle-dragging, turning in
exact lockstep with the cells (because it is driven by the same `pose`/`playShapeAt`) -
giving the eye a small, stable skeleton to read the rotation against instead of 256
independently-moving tiles. Turning `showSeams` off removes it, matching every other
structural (non-gameplay) marking on the shape view.

**Risks and non-goals.** This is a visual aid, not new game state - it must not affect
picking, legality, or anything `CellId`-shaped; a ray should pass through a scaffold edge
exactly as it does today (the existing shape-view pick path tests patches and, once
M17.27 lands, pieces - never this scaffold). Do not fold this into part C's culling: C
removes cell tiles; whether the scaffold's own hidden (far-side) edges should dim or cull
is a separate, smaller follow-up judgement call, not a blocker - a scaffold that is always
fully drawn, with normal depth-testing against the real board geometry, is an acceptable
first cut and arguably still reads fine, the way an uncullled wireframe box usually does.

### Status: D built, but rejected on review - the corners overshoot the real geometry (2026-10-05, Claude)

Built as specified (uncommitted, in the working tree): `hypercubeScaffoldCorners`/
`hypercubeScaffoldEdges` in `play_surface.{hpp,cpp}` call `playShapeAt` at the lattice's
16 extreme corners (`axis[k] in {0, 4}` for all four dims) and wire them up by XOR
adjacency; `buildInstances` emits 32 thin bars on `Archetype::Cell`, gated on `v.name ==
"hyper4" && options_.showSeams`; tests pass (`tools/test.sh --build render`, 135 cases;
`--build app`, 69 cases); the new unit and property tests (16 distinct corners, inner/
outer nesting, degree-4 adjacency, bar orientation, continuity) are all green. All of
that is sound. But a fresh `hyper4 --geometry --shot` shows the scaffold as a handful of
long spikes shooting well past the actual cell-patch stack into the corners of the frame
- not the "two nested cubes around the board" the spec asked for. This is a real defect,
caught by actually looking at the capture rather than trusting the green tests, which
only checked the scaffold's own internal consistency, never its size relative to what is
actually on screen.

**Root cause, found by computing the projection directly (not guessed).** The hyper4
projection only *subdivides* two of the four axes: `buildStacked`'s patch sampling varies
`axis[0]` (file) and `axis[1]` (rank) by up to `kCoverage * 0.5` (`play_surface.hpp`'s
`kCoverage = 0.90f`) around each cell's centre, but `axis[2]` (level) and `axis[3]`
(aeon) are never varied - every real point the renderer ever projects pins them at a
cell's own centre, so `cy` and `cw` (the hyper4 branch's names for them) never leave
`{-1.5, +1.5}`. The scaffold's corners push all four axes out to the lattice's literal
edge, `{-2, +2}`, including `cy`/`cw` - a combination the real geometry never produces.
Because the projection's perspective divide (`k = 2.35 / (3.5 - nd)`) is nonlinear and
`nd` is dominated by the rotated `cw`, pushing `cw` from its real maximum of `1.5` out to
`2.0` is not a small extrapolation: computed directly (`cu = cos(0.9)`, `su = sin(0.9)`,
the shipped default pose), the scaffold's worst corner (`cx=2, cz=±2, cw=2`) gives
`k ≈ 3.405`, while the real geometry's own worst point - the outermost cell's own patch
corner (`cx=1.95, cz=1.95, cw=1.5`, using the actual `kCoverage` inset) - gives only
`k ≈ 2.259`. The scaffold corner is projected about 50% larger than anything the board
itself ever draws, which is exactly the "shoots off past the board" look in the capture.

**The fix, verified numerically before handing back.** Only extend `axis[0]`/`axis[1]`
(file/rank, the two the renderer actually subdivides) to the lattice edge; keep
`axis[2]`/`axis[3]` (level/aeon) at the cell-centre extreme the real geometry actually
uses - `{0.5, 3.5}`, not `{0, 4}`. Recomputed with this change, the scaffold's worst
corner (`cx=2, cz=±2, cw=1.5`) gives `k ≈ 2.348` - within 4% of the real geometry's own
worst point (`2.259`), which is the right ballpark: the scaffold should sit just outside
the cell stack, not far beyond it. Concretely, in `hypercubeScaffoldCorners`, change the
per-axis extreme from the current uniform `axis[d] in {0, 4}` for all four `d` to `{0, 4}`
for `d` in `{0, 1}` (file, rank) and `{0.5, 3.5}` for `d` in `{2, 3}` (level, aeon) -
i.e. the loop needs to know which axis it is on, not apply one constant to all four.

**What this task still needs, before it can land.** Make the fix above. Add the numerical
guard the review above did by hand, as a real test, so this cannot silently regress: the
maximum `k` (or, simpler and not coupled to an internal variable name, the maximum
distance of any scaffold corner from the board's own centroid) across the 16 scaffold
corners must not exceed the maximum distance from centroid of any of the 256 real cell
centres by more than a small, stated factor (e.g. 1.5x - loose enough that the scaffold
visibly encloses the cells, tight enough that today's ~2x-plus overshoot would still fail
it). Re-capture `hyper4 --geometry --shot` after the fix and actually look at it - the
acceptance bar is the same as before: a recognisable two-nested-cubes wire scaffold
*around* the cell patches, not spikes beyond them. Only then build/test/precommit/commit
and append a fresh status note in place of this one (keep this paragraph as the record of
what went wrong and why, the way other corrections in this plan do - do not delete it).
